# Phase 06 — Cleaning, panel construction and data validation

## 1. Objective

Turn the raw downloads into a futures panel and an aligned set of daily series that
can be trusted, and **prove** they can be trusted. End state: `data/interim/` holds
the cleaned panel, every cleaning decision is documented with its justification, and a
data-quality report states what was found and what was done about it.

## 2. Context

This is where most silent errors in this kind of project are introduced. The specific
hazards, from Phase 04:

- Cboe writes `0.0000` for settlement on days a contract had no activity; treating
  that as a price produces a −100% return
- A few files carry rows dated after the contract's own settlement
- The ETP price series need split adjustment, and VXX and UVXY have reverse-split many
  times
- VXX is a two-segment series: the original ETN and the Series B relaunch
- FRED writes `.` for non-observations
- Market holidays differ between the futures and the index series
- Weekly VX contracts share the monthly naming pattern

## 3. Inputs

- `data/raw/**`, `data/raw/_manifest.json`
- `docs/data_sources.md` hazard list
- `svcarry.index.calendar`

## 4. Tasks

1. Parse each raw futures file, attach its expiry, and concatenate into a long panel
   keyed by `(date, expiry)`.
2. Screen prices: set non-positive settlement, open, high, low and close to missing.
   Count and record how many.
3. Drop rows dated after their own expiry; count them.
4. De-duplicate on `(date, expiry)`; count and inspect duplicates before dropping.
5. Restrict to monthly expiries generated by the calendar; confirm no weekly contract
   has entered the panel.
6. Reconcile trading days: compare the panel's dates with the calendar's trading days
   and investigate every difference in both directions.
7. Build the ETP price panel. Compute returns from the provider's adjusted series
   **and** from an independent reconstruction using raw close and recorded split
   factors; compare. A discrepancy is a finding, not a rounding issue.
8. Identify the VXX series break and handle it explicitly.
9. Load and clean the rate series; convert to the accrual convention the design
   specifies.
10. Examine extreme observations. For each of the largest moves, establish whether it
    is a genuine market event, a data error, or an artefact of the construction —
    **before** deciding anything. Do not filter outliers by rule.
11. Write `data/interim/vx_panel.parquet` (or CSV) and the data-quality report.

## 5. Tools and methods

`svcarry.data.cboe.load_vx_panel`, `svcarry.data.prices`, `svcarry.data.fred`;
`svcarry.index.calendar` for the trading-day reconciliation.

## 6. Execution instructions

Never modify raw data in place. Cleaning reads from `data/raw/` and writes to
`data/interim/`.

Do not forward-fill prices to close a gap without saying so. A forward-filled
settlement produces a zero return, which understates volatility and flatters every
risk statistic downstream. If a gap must be filled, record how many observations were
filled and check the effect on the sample's volatility.

Every extreme observation gets a verdict recorded next to it. "Removed as an outlier"
is not a verdict; "5 February 2018, genuine market event, retained" is.

## 7. Validation criteria

| Check | Pass | Warning | Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Row count | matches expiries × their trading lives, reconciled | small unexplained residual, documented | large unexplained residual |
| Missing settlements | < 0.5% of contract-days in the sample, each explained | 0.5–2% | > 2%, or unexplained |
| Duplicates | none after de-duplication; the dropped ones inspected | — | duplicates dropped without inspection |
| Trading days | panel dates ⊆ calendar trading days, differences explained | — | a panel date that is not a trading day |
| Split adjustment | independent reconstruction matches provider within 1 bp/day | matches within 10 bp, investigated | systematic divergence |
| Front-month coverage | a front and second contract priced on ≥ 99% of trading days | 97–99% | < 97% |
| Extremes | every move beyond ±25% has a recorded verdict | — | any such move filtered without a verdict |

## 8. Self-review

- Was anything forward-filled? How many observations, and what did it do to the
  sample standard deviation?
- Does the panel contain any weekly expiry?
- Does the split-adjusted ETP return series contain a single-day move above +50% that
  coincides with a split date? That is the signature of a missed adjustment.
- Is the VXX break handled, or is there a fake return on the relaunch date?
- Would the cleaning decisions be defensible if each one had to be justified
  individually to a sceptical reader? They will have to be.

## 9. Deliverables

- `data/interim/vx_panel.{parquet,csv}`
- `data/interim/etp_prices.csv`, `data/interim/rates.csv`
- a narrative section in `docs/validation.md` with the per-check counts, and the cleaning
  rules in application order in `docs/methodology.md` M1. (This prompt specified a
  `reports/tables/data_quality.csv` and no such table was ever produced; the counts went

- Tests covering the cleaning rules

## 10. Git requirements

Commit the cleaning code, the tests, the data-quality table and the documentation.
Commit interim data only if it is small and redistributable. Commit message states row
counts, the date range and the headline quality findings.

## 11. Completion gate

- [ ] Panel built; row counts reconciled
- [ ] Every screen applied with counts recorded
- [ ] Trading-day reconciliation clean in both directions
- [ ] Split adjustment independently verified
- [ ] VXX series break handled explicitly
- [ ] Every extreme observation has a recorded verdict
- [ ] No undocumented fill, drop or transformation
- [ ] Data-quality report written

## 12. Handoff

Phase 07 consumes the panel and must be told: which days have incomplete front/second
pricing, what was filled and where, and the verdicts on the extreme observations —
because those days are exactly the ones the index reconstruction and the tail model
care most about.
